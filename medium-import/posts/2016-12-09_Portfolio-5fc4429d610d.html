Family members describe mistreatment, poor service at Andrew T. Scheid Funeral Home (LNP)

The Andrew T. Scheid Funeral Home portrays itself as trustworthy and caring, serving mourning families at its Millersville and Lancaster locations with “dignity and reverence.”

Those descriptions do not describe the experience Ryan Stauffer had with the funeral home. He and his wife, Clara, said it took the home more than two years to provide her father’s ashes after his cremation.

“We’ve only had the heartache,” Stauffer, of York, said.

The Stauffers are among more than half a dozen Andrew T. Scheid Funeral Home customers who told LNP | LancasterOnline they, too, have serious concerns about how they were treated.

In interviews conducted over months, some described long, unexplained delays for funeral services. Others alleged more disturbing problems, including neglect of a corpse.

State: York schools led Pa. in sex assault reports in 3 of 10 years (The York Dispatch)

  • Data from the Pennsylvania Department of Education reveals sexual assault incidents over the past 10 years.
  • Over 600 incidents have been reported in schools across Pennsylvania since 2004.
  • 87 of the incidents occurred in York county, 78 of which occurred in the York City School District.

Pa. Capitol staff being paid by taxpayers to edit Wikipedia for elected officials (LNP/The Caucus)

Anyone can edit a Wikipedia entry, but Attorney General Josh Shapiro’s bio is so glowing you’d think one of his staffers wrote it.

Turns out, one of them did.

The flattering edits were made by a digital communications director in the attorney general’s office who gets paid $65,526 a year by taxpayers after a recent raise of nearly $12,000.

The staffer described his boss as a “rising progressive star” who has “earned a reputation as a consensus builder eager to take on the status quo and challenge powerful institutions to protect the people of Pennsylvania.” He said Shapiro is “guided by his faith” and “driven by the teachings of Scripture.”

Rebuilding after domestic violence: Two Lancaster County women tell their stories (LNP)

“Phoebe” has gone through two abusive marriages. “Amber” grew up in a violent home.

Both women eventually began relationships with men they trusted, and in both cases, those men abused them.

Today, Phoebe, 62, and Amber, 23, live in transitional housing for victims of domestic violence as they once again pick up the pieces and try to move on from abusive relationships.

Both women agreed to tell their stories to LNP using pseudonyms so they could bring awareness to the issue of domestic violence.

A year later, Maria evacuees, still reeling, find stability in Lancaster [video] (LNP)

Mildred Santiago teared up several times when speaking about her former home in Juana Diaz on the southern coast of Puerto Rico.

She calls it her former home not only because it’s where she lived but because the structure itself no longer exists.

Less than a year ago, Santiago, 40, was still there with her two young children, ages 13 and 10.

Their rented home was damaged beyond repair and underwater after the storm, so they fled to higher ground at her sister’s home, which sat on a mountainside. They went with only the clothes on their backs, she said.

Turning out in 2016 (The Underground)

The Trump campaign has relied on a different method of voter engagement never seen before.

Beyond Trump using Twitter to connect with more than 12 million people at once, the Republican National Committee has created a mobile app that (Penn State student) Dmitri Loutsik uses to get everything from specific addresses that he needs to reach, to questions and potential responses he can give during an encounter.

All of the information gets immediately added to a national database that will be used for final analysis in the final 12 days of the race, according to Loutsik.

He read the prompt on his iPhone verbatim when encountering his first opened door of the day, an elderly woman.

“Who will you vote for in this upcoming election?” Loutsik asks.

“I’m not supposed to tell people that, am I?” said the woman.

“Well, you can tell me,” said Loutsik, charmingly.

The woman laughed. “I’ll tell you what, I’m not voting for Trump, let’s put it that way,” she said.
